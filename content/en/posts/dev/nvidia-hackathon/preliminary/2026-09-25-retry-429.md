The number I saw most all afternoon on September 25 was HTTP 429 (Too Many Requests).

---

## Background: Infrastructure Failures Look Like Model Failures

When we ran real-data cases of about 50,000 tokens each at 3 per minute, the fourth and fifth failed with 429. The rulebook keeps failures in the denominator too. But `checklist` mode doesn't call the model, so it never gets a 429. Left like this, **a provider-side problem would cut into the model modes' scores.** The comparison wouldn't be fair.

The 429 responses had no headers indicating call limits. Most came right after a 500 error or within a few seconds of one. We took it as a provider processing-capacity issue rather than our call volume (an inference).

## Four Decisions

| Time | Decision | Rejected alternative |
|---|---|---|
| 13:00 | The same rest conditions for every mode: at least 60 seconds between runs, rest proportional to token count, 120 seconds after a 429 | Fixed interval only (not enough after large cases) |
| 15:52 | **Treat 429 like 5xx**: up to 3 retries per request (5, 10, 20 seconds), and once exhausted, rerun as an infrastructure failure at the end of the batch | Just lengthen waits; start as is |
| 18:05 | **Exclude retries from the model request limit** | Raise the limit from 10 to 16 |
| 22:22 | If the server sends `Retry-After`, honor it with a 60-second cap | Request timeout of 120 seconds, 4 retries |

I chose the 15:52 and 18:05 decisions. The first was "Treat 429 like 5xx (recommended)," and the second was "Exclude from the limit (recommended)."

## Why the 18:05 Decision Mattered

Among the runs that didn't finish in the first MVP round, 8 were "model request limit exceeded." That was because retries also counted toward the limit of 10. **Provider-side errors had been disguised as the model exceeding its limit**, and in that state they weren't even eligible for a rerun. Raising the limit was an alternative, but it would only have covered up the problem. Retries were bounded only by 3 per request and 300 seconds per case. The batch in progress was stopped and not scored.

## What This Choice Meant

What these decisions have in common is that they **don't touch the model's verdicts or explanations; they only strip out infrastructure noise.** The same rules apply to every mode, so the comparison conditions stay equal.

## Afterward

The `Retry-After` header never came, not even once. When we analyzed it after the sealed evaluation, 162 of the 466 requests in the holdout40 run (35%) turned out to be 429s; the rejections came back immediately, in about 170 ms, and were spread evenly regardless of time of day. We estimated it was a capacity limit on the shared endpoint. We had no way to confirm the cause from our side. This experience was written into the finals guide as a rule: "Build in 429/5xx handling (retries, excluding retries from the limit, rate pacing) from the very first model-call code."
